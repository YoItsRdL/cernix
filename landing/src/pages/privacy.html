Privacy Policy

Last updated · 2026-11-03

Cernix is a desktop application for Windows, macOS and Linux. It runs entirely on your computer.

We do not operate an application server, run analytics, collect telemetry, or maintain user accounts of the username-and-password variety. When you connect Cernix to your Google Drive (optional), the app talks to Google's API directly from your machine: your photos and metadata go to your own Drive account, not to us. We could not collect them even if we wanted to: there is nowhere for them to land.

Cernix is free and open source. There is no purchase, no licence key, and no payment processor, so there is no billing data to collect. The only external services involved are the ones you opt into (Google Drive) and GitHub, for the update check described below.

This page documents every external request Cernix makes, every OAuth scope it requests, and every byte it writes to your disk. If something on this page is not accurate, that is a bug: please file an issue.

What Cernix reads on your machine

  • 1. Removable drives. When you insert an SD card or USB stick, Cernix's volume watcher reads its file list, EXIF metadata, and chosen files (you decide which). Originals stay on the card unless you explicitly stage them.
  • 2. Your local archive folder. Once you sweep files off a card, Cernix writes them to a date-organised local folder (YYYY / Month / DD) inside its own data folder, in staging/library (the data folder is %APPDATA%\Cernix on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/Cernix on a Mac, ~/.config/Cernix on Linux). These are your photographs, not a cache: they may be the only copy once the card is reformatted.
  • 3. XMP sidecars. Every adjustment you make in the editor is written to a tiny .xmp file next to the RAW. Your RAW pixels are never modified.
  • 4. App-data files. In the data folder: an encrypted token file (auth-tokens.enc, see "What Cernix sends to Google" below); a SQLite ledger of what you have uploaded, so Cernix does not re-push it (cernix-v1.db); your ratings and picks (cernix-ratings.db); your presets (presets.json); and, only if the app crashes at startup, an error log (startup-error.log).
  • 5. Caches. Thumbnails and preset previews, and edit-cache/, where Drive files you open to edit are downloaded to. Safe to delete; they regenerate or download again. When an update is downloaded, the updater also keeps it in a cache of its own, outside the data folder.

What Cernix sends to Google

Drive is opt-in and does nothing until you connect an account. Cernix makes no other request to Google.

1. Google Drive (when you connect your account)

OAuth scopes Cernix requests:

  • drive.file access limited to files Cernix itself creates. It can upload RAW + XMP pairs into its own folder and browse what it put there; the rest of your Drive is invisible to it, including to a compromised copy of the app.
  • userinfo.email the email address linked to your Google account. shown in Settings so you can see which account is connected. Cernix does nothing else with it.
  • userinfo.profile display name and avatar. Cernix reads them with the email but does not display them today, and does nothing else with them.

Tokens received from Google are encrypted with Electron's safeStorage (which delegates to the OS keystore: DPAPI on Windows, the Keychain on a Mac, the desktop keyring on Linux) and written to auth-tokens.enc. They never leave your machine except to be refreshed against Google's own token endpoint. Where the system offers no keystore, Cernix does not save the token and you sign in again next time. The keystore protects the token from other users of the computer; it does not protect it from malware running as you.

When you sync, Cernix uploads the RAW + XMP files you select to the Cernix folder in your Drive. That's the only destination. There is no Cernix server in the loop.

2. Fonts (no request)

The app's typefaces are bundled with it, and this site serves its own. Neither asks Google Fonts or any other host for them, so opening Cernix without connecting Drive makes no request at all, apart from the update check below.

The update check

When it starts, and every six hours while it runs, Cernix asks github.com, where its releases are published, whether there is a newer version. The request is for a small file listing the latest version; when it is newer, Windows and Linux then download the installer from the same place. GitHub sees your IP address, the version you run and your operating system, as it does for anyone who opens a release page. Nothing else is sent: no identifier, no account, no usage data.

Nothing installs without you. A downloaded update waits until you restart Cernix, or until the next time you quit. On a Mac, Cernix only tells you a new version is out and links to it.

Turn it off in Settings under Updates, and Cernix makes no update request at all.

What Cernix never collects or transmits

  • · No analytics. No Google Analytics, no Mixpanel, no Sentry, no PostHog. Open the network panel and you will see the app talk to Google's APIs, and to GitHub for the update check, and nothing else.
  • · No telemetry. Cernix sends zero usage data: no event counts, no feature usage, no error reports.
  • · No crash reporting. If the app crashes, the crash stays on your machine.
  • · No device fingerprinting. Cernix does not generate or transmit a device ID.
  • · No IP logging. Because there is no Cernix server, there is no Cernix access log.
  • · No third-party trackers. This page loads no third-party scripts, fonts, or beacons. The network panel on first load shows requests only against this site's own origin.
  • · No cookies. The desktop app is not a web app; this landing page sets no cookies.

Your data, your control

  • → Disconnect Google Drive. Settings → Google Drive → Disconnect inside the app, which also deletes the sign-in Cernix stored on your computer. You can also revoke Cernix's access directly at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • → Delete all local data. First copy your photographs out of staging/library in Cernix's data folder: it holds everything you swept off a card, and they may be the only copy. Then uninstall Cernix and delete the data folder (%APPDATA%\Cernix on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/Cernix on a Mac, ~/.config/Cernix on Linux). Token, ledger, caches and the archive: all gone.
  • → Files in your Google Drive. They stay until you delete them in Drive. Disconnecting Cernix does not touch them.

Changes to this policy

Material changes will be reflected here with an updated Last updated date at the top. Cernix has no email list, so we cannot notify you of changes: please re-read this page after each app update.
